---
title: "Kilimanjaro - Machame Route"
summary: "One of the main routes up Mount Kilimanjaro, Africa's highest mountain, climbing from Machame Gate through rainforest and moorland to Uhuru Peak and coming down by Mweka."
status: reviewed
---

Machame is one of the most popular ways up Kilimanjaro, steeper and more scenic than the old hut route from Marangu. It starts at the Machame Gate on the south-west side of the mountain, at about 1,800 m, and most operators run it over six or seven days.

The first day climbs through rainforest, often wet and muddy, to the edge of the heath. The second reaches the Shira Plateau, an old collapsed crater with views west to Mount Meru. From there the route traverses below the southern glaciers, climbing to the Lava Tower at about 4,600 m and dropping back to Barranco camp. That pattern of climbing high and sleeping lower is the main reason Machame has a better summit success rate than shorter routes. Next morning the Barranco Wall is a steep scramble on rock steps, using hands but no ropes, followed by a day across ridges and valleys to Karanga and Barafu camp.

The summit push starts around midnight from Barafu. Climbers reach Stella Point on the crater rim around dawn and continue along the rim to Uhuru Peak at 5,895 m, the highest point in Africa. The descent goes down the Mweka route, with a night in the forest zone and an exit at the Mweka gate.

Unlike the Marangu route with its huts, Machame uses tented camps run by licensed operators. Climbers must go with a licensed guide, and porters carry tents, food and water. Park fees are charged per day, so the number of days sets much of the cost. Altitude sickness is the main risk, and a slower itinerary is the best protection against it.

The driest months, and the busiest, are January to early March and June to October. The long rains of April and May make the forest paths slippery and the upper mountain cloudy. The mountain is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
