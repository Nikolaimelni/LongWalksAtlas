---
title: "Te Araroa"
summary: "New Zealand's national trail runs about 3,000 km from Cape Reinga in the far north to Bluff at the southern tip."
status: reviewed
---

Te Araroa runs the length of New Zealand, from Cape Reinga at the northern tip of the North Island to Bluff on the south coast of the South Island. The trail crosses beaches, forests, volcanic landscapes and mountain ranges across both islands and uses a mix of tracks, roads and river and sea crossings. Its name means "the long pathway" in Māori, and it opened as a continuous route in 2011, joining existing tracks with new sections built by local trusts and volunteers.

The North Island half starts along Ninety Mile Beach and passes through Northland's forests, Auckland and the volcanic Tongariro crossing to Wellington, with a paddling section on the Whanganui River that most walkers do by canoe. A ferry crosses Cook Strait. The South Island is wilder: long days in the Richmond Range and the Southern Alps, unbridged rivers and remote huts, then the lakes of Otago and the farmland of Southland before the finish at Stirling Point.

River crossings are the main danger, and the trail is not continuous across two large braided rivers, the Rakaia and the Rangitata, which walkers must go around by road. Many South Island huts belong to the Department of Conservation, and a backcountry hut pass covers most of them. Resupply in the South Island needs planning, with food boxes sent ahead to small settlements.

A thru-hike takes four to six months, and most walkers start at Cape Reinga in spring, between October and December, finishing before autumn weather reaches the southern mountains. Others walk it in sections over several years, or start in the south and walk north.

The Te Araroa Trust manages the trail with regional trusts. A significant share of the route is on roads or farm tracks, and walkers often hitch or skip the busiest road sections.
