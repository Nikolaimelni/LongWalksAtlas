---
title: "Pacific Crest Trail"
summary: "A national scenic trail along the mountains of California, Oregon and Washington from the Mexican border at Campo to Manning Park in Canada, about 4,265 km."
status: reviewed
---

The PCT runs through California, Oregon and Washington, from the Mexican border to just past the Canadian one. It became one of the first two National Scenic Trails in 1968, together with the Appalachian Trail, and was officially completed in 1993. The Pacific Crest Trail Association gives its length as 2,650 miles.

The first 700 miles cross the deserts and dry ranges of Southern California, where water sources can be a day apart and hikers plan around springs, tanks and caches left by volunteers. Then comes the High Sierra: granite passes above 3,500 m, deep snow early in the season, and the trail's highest point at Forester Pass, about 4,000 m. North of the Sierra the route follows the volcanic chain of the Cascades, past Lassen Peak, Crater Lake and Mount Hood, and ends among the glaciers and steep valleys of the North Cascades.

Thru-hikes usually take about five months, and snow and fire conditions dominate planning. Most northbound hikers start at Campo in April or May, time their arrival in the High Sierra for June, when the worst of the snow has gone, and need to reach Canada before autumn storms close the Cascades. Wildfires close sections almost every summer, and smoke can cover long stretches of Oregon and Washington.

The Association issues a free long-distance permit for trips of 500 miles or more. Start dates at the Mexican border are limited to a fixed number each day, and the permits are released online months in advance. California campfire rules and bear canister requirements in parts of the Sierra apply as well.

There are few towns on the trail itself. Hikers hitch or take a shuttle down to resupply points, or have food boxes sent to post offices, resorts and stores near road crossings. The northern terminus is a monument at the border; the trail then continues a few miles to Manning Park in British Columbia.
