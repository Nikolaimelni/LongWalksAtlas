---
title: "Torres del Paine W Trek"
summary: "A five-day trek in Patagonia's Torres del Paine National Park along three valleys shaped like a W: Grey Glacier, the French Valley and the base of the Towers."
status: reviewed
---

The W trek links the Torres, Valle Francés and Grey Glacier, with refugios and campsites in the park. One arm climbs to the lake at the foot of the three granite towers, the middle one runs up the French Valley between hanging glaciers and the dark-capped Cuernos, and the third follows Lago Grey to the glacier front. Paths along the shore of Lago Nordenskjöld connect them.

Walked from east to west, the trek starts near Hotel Las Torres and climbs the Ascencio valley to the Torres viewpoint; the last hour is a steep scramble over moraine boulders. Many walkers do this arm first, as a day trip from the Chileno or Central areas. The middle days follow the lakeshore to the Italiano camp, from where a path climbs to the Británico viewpoint at the head of the French Valley. The route then crosses to Paine Grande on Lago Pehoé and finishes along Lago Grey. It can also be walked the other way, starting with the catamaran across Lago Pehoé.

Most people take four or five days. The ground is not technical, but the days are long and the climbs to the viewpoints add up. Puerto Natales, about two hours away by bus, is the base for supplies and transport into the park.

The Patagonian wind is the main hazard, with gusts strong enough to knock walkers off their feet, and the weather can change several times a day. Beds in refugios and places in the campsites are run by private operators and have to be booked before entering the park; rangers check reservations, and wild camping is not allowed. Fires are strictly banned after large blazes in 2005 and 2011 burned parts of the park.

The walking season is the southern spring and summer, from October to April. December to February has the longest days and the most people; the wind is often strongest then too.
