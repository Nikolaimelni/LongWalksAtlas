---
title: "Nakasendō"
summary: "The old highway between Kyoto and Edo (Tokyo), about 534 km with 69 post towns, partly preserved as a walking trail at Magome and Tsumago."
status: reviewed
---

The Nakasendo was one of the five roads of the Edo period and connected Kyoto and Edo through the mountains. Unlike the coastal Tōkaidō it ran inland, through the valleys of what are now Gifu and Nagano, and travellers rested at post towns with inns, tea houses and official lodgings for feudal lords. In the Edo period there were 69 post towns along the road.

Most of the route now lies under modern roads. The best-preserved part is the Kiso valley, the Kisoji, where eleven post towns lined the river. The walk from Magome to Tsumago, about 8 km over the Magome pass, is the classic short section, with stone paving, cedar forest, waterfalls and a tea house where walkers are offered green tea. Tsumago has been restored so carefully that cars are kept out by day and power lines hidden. Further north, the Torii pass links Yabuhara and Narai, another long street of wooden inns.

Walkers usually stay in traditional inns, with dinner and breakfast included, and send luggage ahead between them; in the main season a service carries bags between the tourist offices of Magome and Tsumago. Many people walk the Kiso section over three to five days, using trains on the JR Chūō line, with stations such as Nakatsugawa, Nagiso and Narai, to skip the stretches on busy roads.

The path is well signposted in English and Japanese. Bear bells hang along the forest sections, and walkers are advised to ring them.

Spring and autumn are the best seasons, with cherry blossom in April and maple colours in November. Summer is hot and humid, and winter brings snow to the higher passes, though the valley towns stay open all year.
