---
title: "MacLehose Trail"
summary: "Hong Kong's longest hiking trail, about 100 km in 10 sections from Pak Tam Chung in Sai Kung to Tuen Mun."
status: reviewed
---

The MacLehose Trail crosses the New Territories through country parks, beaches and mountains, passing the Sai Kung coast, Sha Tin and Tai Mo Shan. It is a government-managed trail and the Oxfam Trailwalker route.

It opened in 1979 and is named after Sir Murray MacLehose, the governor who created Hong Kong's country parks. The first section starts at Pak Tam Chung and follows High Island Reservoir to its East Dam, beside hexagonal volcanic rock columns that are part of a UNESCO Global Geopark. The second section follows some of the territory's best beaches, including the long bays of Tai Long Wan. Further west the trail skirts Ma On Shan and Lion Rock above Kowloon and climbs Tai Mo Shan, at 957 m Hong Kong's highest peak, before descending past reservoirs to Tuen Mun.

The trail is split into 10 sections, which can be walked one at a time. Most start and end at a road with buses or minibuses, so the whole trail can be walked as a series of day hikes from the city. Distance posts every 500 m, numbered from the east, help with navigation and with calling for help in an emergency. Some sections are long and steep, with many steps and little shade.

Government campsites along the route allow a multi-day walk, but water and food are scarce on several sections, so walkers carry plenty. Heat and humidity make summer walking hard, and typhoons can close the country parks. The best season is from October to March, when the air is drier and clearer.

In the Trailwalker event teams of four try to finish the whole trail within 48 hours.
