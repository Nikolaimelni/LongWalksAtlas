---
title: "John Muir Trail"
summary: "A high-altitude Sierra Nevada trail of about 211 miles from Yosemite Valley over passes above 3,500 m to the summit of Mount Whitney."
status: reviewed
---

The John Muir Trail follows the crest of the Sierra Nevada through Yosemite, Kings Canyon and Sequoia National Parks, from Happy Isles in Yosemite Valley to the summit of Mount Whitney, at 4,421 m the highest point in the contiguous United States. The trail is named after the naturalist John Muir, who campaigned for the protection of Yosemite.

For most of its length it shares the path with the Pacific Crest Trail. South of Tuolumne Meadows it climbs over a series of passes above 3,300 m, among them Donohue, Selden, Muir with its stone hut, Mather, Pinchot, Glen and finally Forester Pass, at about 4,000 m the highest point on the PCT. Between the passes the trail drops into forested canyons and past hundreds of lakes. The official end is on Whitney's summit, so walkers still have about 18 km to descend to the trailhead at Whitney Portal.

Every hiker needs a wilderness permit. Most go south from Yosemite, and permits for that start are in heavy demand and allocated by lottery. Bear-resistant food canisters are required along the whole route.

Most hikers need two to three weeks. Resupply is possible at Tuolumne Meadows, Reds Meadow and two remote outposts, Vermilion Valley Resort and Muir Trail Ranch, that hold mailed buckets, after which the last section has no resupply on the trail; walkers either carry food for a week or more or hike out over a side pass such as Kearsarge.

The season usually runs from July to September. In a big snow year the high passes stay icy into July, and stream crossings run high with snowmelt. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in summer, and smoke from wildfires can affect parts of August and September.
