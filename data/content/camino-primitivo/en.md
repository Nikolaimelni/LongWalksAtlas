---
title: "Camino Primitivo"
summary: "The oldest Jacobean route, said to follow the journey of King Alfonso II in the ninth century, leads from Oviedo across the Cantabrian mountains to Santiago."
status: reviewed
---

The Primitivo is the oldest of the routes to Santiago. In the ninth century King Alfonso II of Asturias set out from his capital at Oviedo to visit the newly found tomb of Saint James, and pilgrims have followed his line ever since. It starts at Oviedo cathedral, which keeps an old saying alive: whoever goes to Santiago without visiting San Salvador visits the servant and leaves out the master.

It is a short, hilly road through Asturias and Galicia, quieter than the Francés and with steep climbs. Its best-known stretch is the Hospitales variant, a high and exposed path past the ruins of medieval pilgrim shelters, which should be avoided in fog or snow; a lower route through Pola de Allande is the alternative. Further on the trail passes the dam and reservoir at Grandas de Salime, crosses into Galicia at the Acebo pass and enters Lugo through the city's complete Roman walls, a World Heritage Site.

It joins the Camino Francés at Melide for the last days, and the quiet ends there: the final 50 km into Santiago share the path with the crowds from Sarria.

Albergues, many of them run by local councils, stand at the end of most stages, with private hostels and small hotels in between. Stages are often 25 to 30 km with large height gains, and the long climbs in western Asturias make this a harder walk than its length suggests.

It is usually walked in about 14 stages. Spring and early autumn are the best times, though rain is common in Asturias and Galicia in any season. Walkers carry the pilgrim credential, and the route qualifies for the Compostela.
