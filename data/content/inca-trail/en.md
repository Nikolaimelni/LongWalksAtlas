---
title: "Inca Trail to Machu Picchu"
summary: "The classic Inca road trek over the Andes to Machu Picchu, a four-day walk that finishes at the Sun Gate and the ruins."
status: reviewed
---

The trail follows Inca paths and stone stairways from the Urubamba valley at Km 82, through cloud forest and past ruins such as Runkurakay, Sayacmarca and Wiñay Wayna, to Machu Picchu. Peru's Ministry of Culture manages the route.

Groups usually leave Cusco before dawn and drive to Piscacucho, where the trail crosses the Urubamba on a footbridge. The first day is gentle, past the terraces of Llactapata. The second is the hardest: a long climb to Warmiwañusca, Dead Woman's Pass, at about 4,200 m, followed by a steep descent on stone steps to the Pacaymayo valley. The third day crosses two more passes and the most varied stretch of ruins, ending at the campsite near Wiñay Wayna, with its terraces above the river. On the last morning walkers leave before dawn and reach Inti Punku, the Sun Gate, with the first view down onto the citadel.

Entry is limited by a daily permit quota, which includes guides and porters, so only part of it goes to trekkers. Hikers must go with a licensed operator, and the permits for the dry season often sell out months ahead. The name on the permit must match the passport, which is checked at the start.

Most operators run the trek over four days and three nights, with porters carrying the tents and cooking. A shorter two-day version starts at Km 104 and joins the main trail at Wiñay Wayna. The altitude is lower than many expect on the final day, but the second-day pass comes before most walkers have fully acclimatised, so a few days in Cusco first help.

The dry season from May to September has the clearest views and the most people. The trail closes every February for maintenance, during the heaviest rains.
