---
title: "South West Coast Path"
summary: "Britain's longest National Trail follows the south-west English coast from Minehead round Cornwall to Poole, with more than 1,000 km of cliffs, coves and harbours."
status: reviewed
---

The path hugs the coast through Exmoor, north Devon and Cornwall, rounds Land's End and then runs back east along the Channel to Dorset. It is the longest of England's National Trails and starts in Minehead beside a sculpture of a pair of hands holding a map.

In the north the path climbs over Exmoor's high sea cliffs, then follows the long beaches and headlands of north Devon and Cornwall: Clovelly, Tintagel, Padstow and St Ives. Beyond Land's End it passes the fishing harbours and coves of the south coast, the Lizard, Falmouth and Fowey, the estuaries of south Devon and the Jurassic Coast of East Devon and Dorset, a World Heritage Site for its fossil-rich cliffs. It ends at South Haven Point at the mouth of Poole Harbour.

It has constant short climbs and descents at estuary crossings and headlands, so progress is slower than the distance suggests. Over the whole route the ascent adds up to about 35,000 m, roughly four times the height of Everest. Rivers are crossed by ferries, some running only in summer, and a few estuaries can be waded only at low tide, so walkers check tide tables and timetables. The path is waymarked with the National Trail acorn.

A fast walker needs about 30 days; at a gentler pace it takes seven or eight weeks. Many people walk it in sections over several years. Villages and towns are close together for most of the way, with B&Bs, inns, hostels and campsites, though accommodation is scarce and expensive in August.

Spring and early autumn are the best times. The South West Coast Path Association publishes an annual guide with accommodation lists, ferry times and route changes, and cliff falls lead to frequent diversions.
