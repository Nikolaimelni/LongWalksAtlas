The trek to Everest Base Camp follows the valleys of the Khumbu, home of the Sherpa people, to the camp used by expeditions at the foot of the Khumbu Icefall. Most of the route lies in Sagarmatha National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, among villages, monasteries and some of the highest peaks on Earth.

From the airstrip at Lukla the trail follows the Dudh Koshi river, crossing it on long suspension bridges, to Namche Bazaar, the trading centre of the region. It then climbs past the monastery at Tengboche, with Ama Dablam ahead, to Dingboche or Pheriche, Lobuche and Gorak Shep. The last stretch crosses the moraine of the Khumbu Glacier to the base camp at about 5,360 m. The viewpoint of Kala Patthar above Gorak Shep, about 5,550 m, gives the classic view of Everest, which is hidden from the camp itself.

The Nepal Tourism Board describes a trek of about two weeks that starts and finishes at Lukla, with a gentle two days to Namche and a slow climb beyond it to allow for acclimatisation. Above 3,000 m the usual advice is to raise your sleeping height by no more than a few hundred metres a day and to spend extra nights in Namche and Dingboche. Headaches and breathlessness are common; walkers who get worse should go down.

Lodges, known as teahouses, are found in every village, with simple rooms and a heated dining room. Prices rise with the altitude, because everything above the airstrip is carried in by porters or yaks. A national park entry permit and a local area permit are needed, and Nepal's rules on permits and guides have changed several times in recent years, so check them before you go.

The flights to Lukla are short and spectacular but often delayed by cloud, and in the busy seasons many depart from Ramechhap rather than Kathmandu. October and November give the clearest skies, followed by March to May. Some walkers add the Gokyo lakes or the high Cho La pass for a longer loop.
