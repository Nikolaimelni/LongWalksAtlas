---
title: "Appalachian Trail"
summary: "The United States' most famous long-distance footpath runs about 2,200 miles along the Appalachian ridges from Springer Mountain in Georgia to Katahdin in Maine, through 14 states."
status: reviewed
---

The idea came from the forester Benton MacKaye in 1921, and volunteer clubs finished the footpath in 1937. It is still marked with white blazes and largely maintained by volunteers, under the Appalachian Trail Conservancy and the National Park Service.

The trail crosses 14 states on its way from Georgia to Maine. It follows the long ridges of the Appalachians through the Great Smoky Mountains, where it reaches its highest point on Clingmans Dome at 2,025 m, then the Blue Ridge of Virginia and Shenandoah National Park. Further north come the rocky ground of Pennsylvania, the White Mountains of New Hampshire, the hardest terrain on the whole route, and the lakes and forests of Maine. Near the end the Hundred-Mile Wilderness has no road crossings with services, so walkers carry food for a week or more.

More than 260 shelters stand along the way, three-sided wooden lean-tos usually a day's walk or less apart, with campsites nearby. Thru-hikers resupply in small trail towns where the path crosses a road, or have boxes mailed ahead. Great Smoky Mountains National Park asks for a backcountry permit, and Baxter State Park in Maine has its own permit for long-distance hikers who finish on Katahdin.

Most thru-hikers go north from Springer Mountain between late February and early April and take about six months. The early start spreads them thinly, but the busiest weeks still crowd the shelters in Georgia and North Carolina, so the Conservancy encourages southbound or "flip-flop" hikes. Katahdin closes to hikers in mid-October, which sets the deadline for northbound walkers. Southbounders start in Maine in June or July, after the worst of the black flies.

Most people never walk the whole trail. Day hikers and section hikers use it far more than thru-hikers do. Sections are rerouted now and then, so the length shifts a little from year to year.
