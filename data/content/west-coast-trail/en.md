---
title: "West Coast Trail"
summary: "A rugged coastal trail on the west coast of Vancouver Island, 75 km of beaches, ladders and rainforest between Pachena Bay and Gordon River."
status: reviewed
---

The route was originally a lifesaving trail for shipwreck survivors, built after the loss of the SS Valencia in 1906 on a coast known as the Graveyard of the Pacific, and is now a strenuous hike in the Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It runs along the south-west coast of Vancouver Island between Pachena Bay near Bamfield and Gordon River near Port Renfrew.

It crosses the territories of the Huu-ay-aht, Ditidaht and Pacheedaht First Nations and uses ladders, cable cars and suspension bridges over the gullies and rivers. The northern part is easier, with long stretches of beach and the Pachena Point lighthouse. The southern part is rougher, with mud, roots, fallen trees and long ladder climbs through dense rainforest. Highlights include Tsusiat Falls, which drops straight onto the beach, the sea caves and arches along the shore and the Carmanah Point lighthouse.

Some beach sections and headlands can be passed only at low tide, so hikers carry a tide table and plan each day around it. Small ferries run by First Nations cross the Gordon River at the southern end and the Nitinaht Narrows in the middle. Hikers camp at designated beach campsites, usually beside a creek, and carry all their food; there is no resupply on the trail.

Hikers must reserve and attend an orientation, and the trail is open only from 1 May to 30 September. Reservations open early in the year and the summer dates fill quickly. Besides the two ends, there is a mid-point entry at Nitinaht Village for those who want to walk half the trail.

Most people take six to eight days. Rain is possible in any month, the boardwalks and ladders are slippery when wet, and injuries are common enough that the park runs regular evacuations. Buses and shuttles link the trailheads with Victoria and Nanaimo.
