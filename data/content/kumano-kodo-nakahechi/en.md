---
title: "Kumano Kodo - Nakahechi"
summary: "The main Kumano Kodo pilgrim route in Japan's Kii Peninsula, from Takijiri to the Kumano Hongu Taisha shrine, usually walked in two to four days."
status: reviewed
---

The Nakahechi was the main road for emperors and commoners going to the three Kumano shrines and runs over forested passes with small waystation shrines (oji). The route is part of the UNESCO-listed Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range.

From Takijiri, traditionally the entrance to the sacred mountains, the path starts with a steep climb through cedar forest to the village of Takahara. It then follows ridges and old hamlets past Gyuba-doji and Chikatsuyu, crosses a series of small passes and reaches Hosshinmon-oji, the outer gate of the Hongu shrine area. The last few kilometres descend through tea fields and forest to Kumano Hongu Taisha. Nearby stands the giant torii gate at Oyunohara, the shrine's original site on a sandbank in the river.

Most walkers take two days to Hongu with a night in Chikatsuyu or Takahara, then continue by trail, bus or river boat to the other two grand shrines, Hayatama Taisha in Shingu and Nachi Taisha beside Nachi Falls, at 133 m the tallest single-drop waterfall in Japan. The Daimon-zaka, a stone stairway lined with old cedars, is the classic approach to Nachi.

Lodging is in small family inns, minshuku, which serve dinner and breakfast, and beds are limited, so booking ahead is essential; the Tanabe City Kumano Tourism Bureau runs a reservation service for the route. Luggage transfer between inns is widely used. Numbered markers every 500 m keep navigation simple. The hot spring village of Yunomine, near Hongu, has the Tsuboyu bath, the only hot spring included in the World Heritage listing.

The route starts from a bus stop reached from Kii-Tanabe station. Spring and autumn are the best seasons; the rainy season in June and typhoons in late summer can close sections. The route is twinned with the Camino de Santiago, and walkers who complete both can register as dual pilgrims.
