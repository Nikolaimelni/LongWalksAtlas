---
title: "GR20"
summary: "The GR20 is a demanding high-mountain footpath that crosses Corsica from Calenzana in the north-west to Conca in the south-east along the island's granite spine."
status: reviewed
---

The GR20 crosses Corsica from north to south along its granite spine, from Calenzana in the Balagne to Conca near Porto-Vecchio. It is often described as one of Europe's hardest long paths because of rough ground and variable weather, even though the stages are short.

The trail follows the central ridge of the island, with rock scrambles, chains and ladders on steep sections and long stretches of boulders and scree. It is waymarked with red and white stripes. The northern half, through the high country around Monte Cinto, is the harder and wilder part. The section through the Cirque de la Solitude was closed after a rockfall killed seven walkers in 2015, and the official route now climbs over the shoulder of Monte Cinto instead. The village of Vizzavona, roughly halfway, has a station on the railway between Ajaccio and Bastia, so many people walk one half at a time. The southern half is somewhat gentler, with beech and pine forest, the Coscione plateau and the needles of Bavella near the end.

A typical day still means about seven hours of walking. Walkers sleep in mountain refuges run by the Corsica regional natural park, or camp beside them; every night, even a tent pitch, has to be booked online in advance. Some refuges sell basic food, and shepherds' huts along the way sell cheese and charcuterie, but supplies are limited and walkers carry several days' food on some sections.

Conditions are good from June to mid-October. July and August are crowded and hot, and afternoon thunderstorms build quickly over the ridge, so walkers start at dawn. Snow can block the high passes in early June.

The route takes most people about two weeks, or 16 stages on the standard plan. Calenzana is a short drive from Calvi, which has an airport, a ferry port and trains.
