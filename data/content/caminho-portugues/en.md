---
title: Portuguese Way
summary: The pilgrim road from Lisbon through Coimbra and Porto to the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela.
status: reviewed
---

Pilgrims have walked north from Lisbon to Santiago since the Middle Ages. Most start in Porto, a little over 240 km from Santiago; the full road from Lisbon is about 620 km.

South of Porto the route follows the Tagus valley and then crosses farmland through Santarém, the Templar town of Tomar and the university city of Coimbra, with long stretches on minor roads. Pilgrim hostels are sparse on this part, and walkers often use small hotels and guesthouses.

North of Porto walkers choose between the central route inland through Barcelos and Ponte de Lima, which enters Spain over the bridge from Valença to Tui, and a coastal variant along the Atlantic through Vila do Conde and Viana do Castelo that rejoins it at Redondela. The central route has one real climb, over the Serra da Labruja north of Ponte de Lima. In Galicia the two lines run together through Pontevedra and Padrón, where tradition says the boat carrying the body of Saint James came ashore. From Pontevedra the Spiritual Variant leads through the monastery of Armenteira and down the Ría de Arousa by boat.

The waymarking uses yellow arrows, and blue arrows for walkers heading the other way to Fátima. North of Porto there is a dense chain of albergues and private hostels.

From Tui to Santiago is about 119 km, just enough for the pilgrim certificate, so this last stretch is the busiest part of the route. Walkers carry a credential and need two stamps a day over the last 100 km. Spring and early autumn are the usual seasons; the coast is cooler than the inland route in summer.
