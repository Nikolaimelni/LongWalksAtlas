title: "Shikoku Pilgrimage"
summary: "A circular pilgrimage of 88 temples around the island of Shikoku, about 1,200 km, associated with the monk Kūkai."
status: reviewed
---

The Shikoku Henro is the best-known circular pilgrimage in Japan, a loop of 88 Buddhist temples around the island of Shikoku associated with the monk Kūkai, known as Kōbō Daishi. It is walked clockwise starting from temple 1 at Ryōzen-ji, and many pilgrims walk it in sections.

The four prefectures of the island stand for four stages on the path: awakening in Tokushima, discipline in Kōchi, enlightenment in Ehime and nirvana in Kagawa. Kōchi is the long, empty stretch, with days of walking along the Pacific coast between temples and the capes of Muroto and Ashizuri. Elsewhere the route mixes mountain temples reached by steep forest paths with long sections on roads through towns and rice fields.

At each temple pilgrims pray at the main hall and the Daishi hall and have their book stamped and inscribed with calligraphy at the temple office. Traditional pilgrim dress is a white vest, a sedge hat and a staff, which stands for Kōbō Daishi himself; "dōgyō ninin", two travelling together, is written on many hats. White clothing and a staff are traditional, but they are not required for walkers today.

Walking the whole circuit at once takes most people six to eight weeks. Pilgrims sleep in temple lodgings, family inns and business hotels, and some use free huts and rest shelters built for walkers. Red arrows and small pilgrim signs mark the walking route. Local people still offer osettai, small gifts of food, drink or a bed, to walking pilgrims, and accepting them is part of the custom.

The circuit ends at temple 88, Ōkubo-ji, though many pilgrims go back to temple 1 to close the loop, and some finish with a visit to Kōyasan, where Kōbō Daishi's mausoleum stands. Spring and autumn are the main seasons. Summer is hot and humid, and typhoons can arrive in September.
