---
title: "Chilkoot Trail"
summary: "A historic gold rush route from Dyea, Alaska over the Chilkoot Pass to Lake Bennett in British Columbia, 33 miles long."
status: reviewed
---

In 1897-98 prospectors carried tons of supplies over the pass on their way to the Klondike. Canadian authorities made each of them bring roughly a year's provisions, about a ton of goods, so most climbed the last steep slope to the summit dozens of times. Rusted stoves, boots and the remains of tramways and camps still lie beside the trail.

Today the trail is managed jointly by the US National Park Service and Parks Canada as part of the Klondike Gold Rush international historic park. It starts at Dyea, near Skagway in Alaska, and climbs from coastal rainforest along the Taiya River. The last section to the pass is a steep climb over boulders, the Golden Stairs of the gold rush photographs, to the summit at about 1,070 m, which is also the border. On the Canadian side the trail descends past a string of lakes into the dry boreal forest of British Columbia and ends at Bennett, on the lake where the stampeders built boats to float down to Dawson City.

The number of hikers crossing the pass is limited by permit, to 50 a day. Permits are booked through Parks Canada, and every hiker attends an orientation at the trail centre in Skagway before starting. Hikers camp only at designated campgrounds, and must report to customs on entering Canada.

Most people take three to five days. The trail is open and staffed in summer, from early June to early September. Snow can cover the upper slopes well into July, and fog and rain are common at the pass. Black and grizzly bears live along the route, and food is kept in bear-proof lockers at the campgrounds.

There is no road to Bennett. Hikers return by the White Pass and Yukon Route railway, which runs on certain days in summer, or walk out to the highway at Log Cabin, or take a floatplane.
