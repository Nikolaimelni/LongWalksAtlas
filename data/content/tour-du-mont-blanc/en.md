---
title: Tour du Mont Blanc
summary: A loop around the Mont Blanc massif through France, Italy and Switzerland, classically starting and ending in Les Houches.
status: reviewed
---

The route crosses a string of high passes between the valleys of Chamonix, Courmayeur and Champex, sleeping in mountain huts and village inns along the way. It is usually walked anticlockwise from Les Houches, and over the whole loop the climbing adds up to about 10,000 m.

The first days lead over the Col de Voza and through the Contamines valley to the Col du Bonhomme, where snow can linger into July. The Col de la Seigne leads from France into Italy, with the south face of Mont Blanc and the Val Veny below. From Courmayeur the path follows the Val Ferret under the Grandes Jorasses and crosses the Grand Col Ferret into Switzerland. From Champex it crosses to Trient, by the Bovine path or the steeper Fenêtre d'Arpette, then climbs back to France over the Col de Balme, with the Aiguilles of Chamonix ahead, and finishes along the balcony paths opposite the Mont Blanc massif.

Most stages are between 15 and 20 km with 1,000 m or more of climbing, on well-marked paths with no scrambling on the main line. Several harder variants cross higher ground, and buses and cable cars make it easy to shorten a day or skip a valley. Les Houches is a short bus or train ride from Chamonix, which has transfers from Geneva airport.

Huts are busy in July and August and need booking well ahead; many of them can be reserved through the official Autour du Mont Blanc site. The usual season runs from mid-June to mid-September, when the passes are mostly free of snow and the refuges are open. Early in June some high sections still need an ice axe, and afternoon thunderstorms are common in summer, so the higher passes are best crossed in the morning.

The loop is also the course of the Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc, the trail race held each August, which fills the valleys for a week.
