---
title: "Hadrian's Wall Path"
summary: "A National Trail along the Roman frontier between Wallsend on the Tyne and Bowness-on-Solway, passing forts, milecastles and the crags of the Whin Sill."
status: reviewed
---

The route crosses northern England coast to coast, mostly along or close to the line of the second-century wall built on the orders of Emperor Hadrian. It became a National Trail in 2003 and runs from Wallsend on the Tyne, beside the Roman fort of Segedunum, to Bowness-on-Solway on the Solway Firth.

From Newcastle the path follows the river and then climbs west through farmland where the wall survives mostly as earthworks. The central section over the Whin Sill crags offers the best preserved remains, with forts such as Housesteads and Vindolanda a short distance from the path. Here the wall rides the edge of north-facing cliffs, with turrets and milecastles along the ridge; Sycamore Gap, where a famous tree stood until it was felled illegally in 2023, is in this stretch. West of Birdoswald the remains fade again, and the last day crosses the flat salt marshes of the Solway coast.

Most walkers take six or seven days. Accommodation is in villages and farms near the line of the wall and in towns such as Hexham, Haltwhistle and Carlisle. In summer the AD122 bus runs along the central section, and trains between Newcastle and Carlisle make it easy to reach any part. A passport stamping scheme runs from May to October for walkers who want a record of the walk.

The wall is part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and walkers are asked to stay off it and to keep away in the wettest months, when the grass over buried remains wears through easily. The path is gentle by the standards of British long-distance walks, with the only real climbs in the central crags.

The season runs from May to October. Winter walking is possible, but the trail asks walkers to keep to hard surfaces and avoid wet grassland until spring.
