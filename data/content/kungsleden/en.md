---
title: "Kungsleden"
summary: "Sweden's famous Arctic hut-to-hut trail runs about 450 km from Abisko above the Arctic Circle to Hemavan in the south, through Lapland's mountains and valleys."
status: reviewed
---

Kungsleden, the King's Trail, runs through Swedish Lapland from Abisko in the north to Hemavan in the south. It is run by the Swedish Tourist Association, which maintains huts, bridges and boat transfers.

Its best-known section is the north, from Abisko through the Tjäktja pass to Nikkaluokta, where walkers cross open tundra and alpine valleys beneath Sweden's highest mountains. A side branch leads to the Kebnekaise mountain station, the base for climbing Sweden's highest mountain. South of Saltoluokta the trail runs along the edge of Sarek and the Laponia World Heritage area, the homeland of the Sámi, whose reindeer graze across the hills. The middle section between Kvikkjokk and Ammarnäs is the quietest, with long distances between huts. The southern end through Ammarnäs to Hemavan crosses gentler hills with birch forest.

In the north the huts stand roughly a day's walk apart and sell basic food in summer, so walkers can travel without a tent. Further south the huts thin out and some days need a tent or a long walk. Several lakes are crossed by boat: some by motorboat in the main season, others in rowing boats kept at each shore. If only one boat is on your side, you row across, tow one back and row over again, so that a boat is always left on each side.

The summer season, when the huts are staffed, runs from late June to mid-September. Mosquitoes are worst in July; late August brings autumn colours and the first frosts. In March and April the trail becomes a ski route, marked with red crosses on poles.

Abisko is reached by train from Stockholm, including an overnight sleeper, and Hemavan has flights and buses south. The exact distance depends on which side trips you add and how many of the lake crossings you make by boat.
