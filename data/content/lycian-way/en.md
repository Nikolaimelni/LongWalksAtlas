---
title: Lycian Way
summary: A waymarked footpath along the coast of ancient Lycia in southern Turkey, from Ölüdeniz near Fethiye to Geyikbayırı near Antalya.
status: reviewed
---

Laid out and waymarked in 1999 by Kate Clow, the path links old mule tracks and Roman roads past the tombs and theatres of Lycian cities, with long stretches high above the sea. It starts at Ölüdeniz near Fethiye and follows the coast of the Teke peninsula east to the mountains above Antalya.

The first days climb above the lagoon at Ölüdeniz and Butterfly Valley to villages such as Faralya and Kabak. The path then passes the UNESCO-listed sites of Xanthos and the Letoon, the ruins and long beach at Patara, the harbour town of Kaş and the sunken city and castle at Kekova. Further east come Myra with its rock tombs, the pine forests above Finike and Olympos, and the natural gas flames of the Chimaera. The final section turns inland and climbs into the mountains west of Antalya before ending near Geyikbayırı.

The path is marked with red and white stripes and is rocky and steep in many places, with few villages on some stages. Water is the main concern away from the coast: cisterns and springs exist but are not always reliable, so walkers carry more than they think they need. Pensions and village guesthouses cover most stages, and camping fills the gaps. Kate Clow's guidebook and the trail's GPS tracks are the usual navigation aids.

Summer heat makes it hard going, so most people walk in spring or autumn, often in sections; March to May and October to November are best. The whole route takes about five weeks. Airports at Dalaman and Antalya serve the two ends, and buses along the coast road make it easy to join or leave the path.

The route has been extended several times since it opened, and the distance depends on which variants you count.
