---
title: "Annapurna Circuit"
summary: "A classic Himalayan trek around the Annapurna massif through the Thorong La pass, from Besisahar to Nayapul."
status: reviewed
---

The circuit crosses climate zones from subtropical valleys to the high desert of Manang and the 5,416 m Thorong La pass. It usually goes anticlockwise: up the Marsyangdi valley with Manaslu and the Annapurnas in view, over the pass to Muktinath, a temple town sacred to both Hindus and Buddhists, and then south down the deep Kali Gandaki gorge below Dhaulagiri.

The lower valley climbs through rice terraces, waterfalls and Gurung and Manangi villages. Above Chame the landscape turns dry and Tibetan in character, with prayer walls and monasteries around Pisang and Manang. A popular side trip from Manang leads to Tilicho Lake, one of the highest large lakes in the world. Most trekkers spend one or two acclimatisation days around Manang before the pass, then sleep at Thorong Phedi or High Camp and start the crossing before dawn, when the wind is lighter.

On the western side the trail descends past Kagbeni and the walled town of Jomsom, where many walkers end with a flight or bus to Pokhara. Others continue down the Kali Gandaki to the hot springs at Tatopani and climb to Ghorepani and the viewpoint of Poon Hill before finishing at Nayapul.

Roads now reach much of the valley floor on both sides, so walkers either use the side paths marked to avoid the traffic or take a jeep for some stretches. Teahouses offer rooms and meals in every village. A permit for the Annapurna Conservation Area is required. In 2023 Nepal introduced a rule that foreign trekkers in conservation areas such as this one must go with a licensed guide; check the current rules before you go.

Allow three weeks or more. Spring and the clear weeks after the monsoon, from late September to November, are the usual seasons. In winter the pass is often closed by snow, and the monsoon brings leeches and landslides to the lower valleys.
